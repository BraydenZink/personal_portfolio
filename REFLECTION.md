Instead I just asked it to do a similar process to what it helped me do in ICE 5 and it did it all by copying and pasting commands in the terminal. I broke this project down into multiple prompts. I did begin with a larger prompt kind of telling the AI what I was doing and also what was required. I did not copy and paste the rubric but I did summarize parts of. I made sure it knew what components needed included as well told it the process of deployment so that it knew. Then I made sure it knew what needed to be in the repo. After that it would plan and ask me to approve which I would as mentioned earlier. It would then ask me for my personal information to include. I was really satisfied with how accurate and well it did with the entire user interface. All of the buttons worked and the information was all correct. The only thing I think it could have done better in a sense was doing a better job at pulling info from my resume which I provided it and applying it. I would need to tell it to include certain parts to boost the certain pages. Thus, my prompting strategy was start bigger and more general and then as the project went on the prompts got much smaller and much more specific much faster. When judging the AI generated code, I didn't really look at the code too closely because I trusted that it was correct. I personally don't know how to code too well so I that is why I didn't look at it that close either. Rather I focused more of my attention on the results in terms of what the pages looked like and the content included in addition to making sure it satisfied requirements. For future projects I will make sure that I do ask the AI what it is doing for the code. I don't want to blindly follow what it gives me for code.
